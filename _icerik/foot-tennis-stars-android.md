For a while, the most common question about Foot Tennis Stars was a simple one: *is it on Android?* As of today, the answer is yes. Version 2.0 is live on Google Play, and it's the same game as on iPhone, down to the last arena.

## Three days, two stores

It has been a fast week. On Saturday, version 2.0 went to Apple and was approved the same day. On Sunday, Google approved the game for production after its closed test. That night I sent version 1.9.1 to Google Play, just to get the game out to Android players. Today, I sent 2.0 to Google, and it was approved and live within the same day.

So in three days, Foot Tennis Stars went from an iPhone-only game to a game you can play on almost any phone.

## iPhone and Android, in the same match

This is the part I'm proudest of. Online Quick Match doesn't care what phone you have. An Android player and an iPhone player can end up on the same court, in the same rally.

The lobby shows the real number of players searching right now. If nobody is around, it tells you a bot rival is ready instead of making you wait or pretending there's a crowd. No fake numbers.

You can also play with a friend: one of you hosts a room, the other types in the code, and you're in the same match, 1 v 1 or 2 v 2.

## What you get in 2.0

Android players start with everything iPhone players got this weekend:

- **Nine rebuilt arenas.** A floodlit stadium, an indoor hall with a polished parquet floor, a beach at sunset, a rooftop above the city at night, a snowy peak, a desert and more.
- **The Nations Cup.** Sixteen nations, one trophy. The camera sweeps over the stadium, your team walks out of the tunnel, and if you win the final, you lift the cup.
- **A new opening.** Every time you start the game, you dive into the stadium.
- **Your name and number** on the back of your kit.
- **Smoother play.** Before 2.0 shipped, I tracked down a freeze that stopped rallies for a second at a time. It was one deleted part still hiding inside a player's skeleton. It's gone.

The game runs on Android 8.0 and newer, and it's free to download.

## The same store page, in 39 languages

The Google Play page now matches the App Store: 39 languages, the same descriptions and the same 2.0 screenshots, in Turkish for Turkish players and in English for everyone else. A few languages still use the English description, and I'll translate those next.

## What's next

Android is a big step for a solo studio. Foot Tennis Stars is the first of my games on both stores, and it won't be the last. If you're on Android, I'd love to hear how it runs on your phone: the online lobby, the frame rate, anything that feels off. Every review on Google Play helps a small game get found.

See you on the court.
