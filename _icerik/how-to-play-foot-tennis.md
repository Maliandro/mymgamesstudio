**Foot tennis** (also called football tennis or soccer tennis) is a net game played with a football: two sides, a net about 1.1 metres high, and no hands. You can use your feet, thighs, chest and head to control the ball and send it back over the net. Each side gets a limited number of touches and may let the ball bounce once. The game started in Prague in the 1920s, when footballers began kicking a ball over a rope, and today it is played as singles, doubles and triples.

This guide explains the rules of the real sport, how a match is scored, and a few tips for your first games. At the end we show how our mobile game, [Foot Tennis Stars](/games/foot-tennis-stars/), turns those rules into something you can play with your thumbs.

## The rules in 30 seconds

- **No hands or arms.** Any other part of the body may touch the ball.
- **Touches:** singles players get up to **2** touches per side; doubles and triples teams get up to **3**.
- **Bounces:** the ball may bounce **once** on your side. A second bounce loses the point.
- **In doubles and triples**, the same player may not touch the ball twice in a row. In singles you may.
- **Every rally scores a point.** The side that wins the rally serves next.
- **A set goes to 11 points** with a two-point lead (the rulebook caps it at 15). A match is **best of three sets**.
- **You lose the point** if the ball lands out after your touch, if you touch the net, or if the ball touches your arm or hand.

Rules differ slightly between federations and competitions. The details below follow a national rulebook translated from the international federation's rules, the federation's tour regulations and an encyclopedia summary (see the sources at the end).

## The court, the net and the ball

- **Court:** 9 m wide and 12.8 m long for singles and doubles, split by the net into two halves of 9 × 6.4 m. Triples use a longer court, 9 × 18 m. The lines count as part of the court.
- **Service zones:** in singles and doubles, a centre line splits each half lengthwise into two boxes of 4.5 × 6.4 m, like a small tennis court.
- **Net:** the top of the net is set between **1.08 and 1.12 m** (so about 1.1 m). For women and junior players the rulebook lowers it to 1.00 m.
- **Ball:** a football with 32 panels, 396–453 g and 680–710 mm around, so roughly a standard size 5 ball. Younger children may use a lighter ball.

At home you don't need any of this. A badminton or volleyball net set low, a chalk line and any football will do.

## Singles, doubles and triples

- **Singles:** one player per side. The two-touch limit makes singles a game of control: a touch to settle the ball, a second to send it over.
- **Doubles:** two players per side, up to three touches between them, and no player may touch twice in a row. The usual pattern is *receive, set, attack*, like volleyball with your feet.
- **Triples:** three players per side on the longer court, with the same three-touch rule.

There are also mixed categories, and juniors may be allowed an extra bounce.

## How to serve

1. Stand **behind the baseline**. Neither you nor the ball may touch the line while you serve.
2. Kick the ball with any body part except your arms and hands. You can drop it from your hand and kick it in the air, kick it after it bounces, or kick it straight off the ground.
3. Serve within **5 seconds** of the referee's whistle.
4. The serve must **cross the net and land in the receiver's service zone** (or hit the receiver). A serve that clips the top of the net and still lands in is good.
5. In singles and doubles the serve goes **diagonally**, like in tennis: from the right when the server's score is even, from the left when it is odd. In triples the serve may land anywhere in the opponent's service zone, the part of their half nearest the net.
6. **Whoever wins the rally serves next.**

## Playing a rally

- After the ball crosses the net, your side may let it **bounce once** and use its touches (2 in singles, 3 in doubles and triples) to send it back.
- At least one touch is needed before the ball goes back over. If you receive a serve **in the air**, your side needs at least two touches.
- The ball **can't go over the net straight off a bounce**: someone has to play it.
- You may reach over to the other side to play the ball only if you take off from, and land on, your own half.
- Touching the net with any part of your body loses the point.

## Scoring a match

Foot tennis uses rally scoring: every rally ends in a point for one side, whoever served. A **set** is played to **11 points**, and you must win by two. The rulebook caps a set at 15 points; some professional tours instead play one deciding point at 12–12. The first side to win **two sets** wins the match. Between sets there is a one-minute break, and each side can call short time-outs.

## Ways to lose a point

- The ball bounces twice on your side.
- Your side uses too many touches, or the same player touches twice in a row (doubles and triples).
- The ball touches a hand or arm.
- You touch the net.
- Your shot lands outside the opponent's half, or passes outside the width of the net.
- Your serve misses the service zone, or you step on the baseline while serving.
- Indoors: your shot hits the ceiling.

## Tips for your first games

- **Use your first touch to slow the ball down.** A soft touch with the thigh or the inside of the foot buys you time. In singles, that gives you one touch to place the ball over the net.
- **Let it bounce when you are unsure.** The one bounce you are allowed is there to help you; a ball taken early in the air is harder to control.
- **Aim deep and wide.** A ball that lands near the back corners forces your opponent to move and makes their first touch harder.
- **Lob a player who stands at the net,** and smash a high ball that drops near the net on your side.
- **In doubles, talk.** Call "mine" or "yours" early, and decide who sets and who attacks before the point starts.
- **Practise keepy-uppies.** Ball control with the thigh, foot and head is most of the game.
